Fancy Fonts for Instagram, TikTok, WhatsApp and Discord

None of these apps has a font picker, so styled text is always pasted in as Unicode. Where you can paste it depends on the app.

Instagram

Styled text works in your bio, in the Name field under Edit Profile, in captions and in comments. It does not work in your username, which only allows letters, numbers, periods and underscores. The bio limit is 150 characters, and some apps count styled letters as more than one character, so leave a little room. Search matches plain letters, so a fancy Name may not show up when people search for you with normal text. Keep your username easy to recognize.

TikTok

Paste styled text into your display name, your bio and your captions. Your @username only accepts plain letters, numbers, underscores and periods, so the fancy style belongs in the display name. Names and bios are short, so pick a readable style such as Bold or Sans Bold.

WhatsApp

You can paste styled text into your profile name, your About line, your status and your group names. WhatsApp also has built-in formatting for messages: asterisks around a word make it bold, underscores make it italic and tildes strike it through. That built-in formatting only works inside messages, so use Font Craft for names and About text.

Discord

Styled text works in your display name, server nicknames, About Me, custom status and messages. The unique username only allows lowercase letters, numbers, underscores and periods. Text channel names are lowercased and hyphenated by Discord, so styled letters may not survive there. Bubble and Small Caps are popular choices for nicknames.

How Fancy Fonts Work

Fancy fonts are not real fonts. The Unicode standard contains many sets of letter-like symbols, such as mathematical bold, script, fraktur, circled and fullwidth letters. Font Craft swaps each normal letter or number for its look-alike symbol. The result is plain text, so it can be copied, pasted and sent like any other message.

Tips and Limits

  • Keep names readable. Bold, Sans Bold and Small Caps stay clear at small sizes. Thin styles like Script are harder to read on phones.
  • Use one style at a time. Mixing several styles in one name looks messy.
  • Know what gets converted. Only the letters A–Z and the digits 0–9 are converted. Accented letters, emoji and other alphabets stay as they are, and a few styles have no number symbols, so digits stay plain in those.
  • Think about accessibility. Screen readers can read these symbols as odd characters or skip them, so keep important information in plain text.
  • Leave room under character limits. Some apps count styled letters as more than one character.
