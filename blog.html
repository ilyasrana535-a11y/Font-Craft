Changing Fonts on Instagram, TikTok, WhatsApp and Discord

Instagram

Open Edit Profile and paste your styled text into the Name field or the Bio. Captions and comments accept styled text too. Your username is the exception: it only allows letters, numbers, periods and underscores. The bio limit is 150 characters, and some apps count styled letters as more than one character, so leave a few spare. Keep your plain username easy to recognize, because search matches plain letters and may not find a fancy Name. Plan your whole bio line by line with the Instagram Bio Fonts builder.

TikTok

Go to Edit Profile and paste the styled text into your Name or Bio. Captions work as well. Your @username only accepts letters, numbers, underscores and periods. Names and bios are short, so Bold or Sans Bold usually reads best. For a decorated name, try the Aesthetic Title Generator.

WhatsApp

Paste styled text into your profile name, your About line and your status. WhatsApp also has its own formatting for messages: put asterisks around a word for bold, underscores for italic, tildes for strikethrough and three backticks for monospace. Use Font Craft where that built-in formatting does not apply, such as names and About text. The WhatsApp Font Changer adds the formatting for you and shows a live preview.

Discord

Paste styled text into your display name, server nickname, About Me and custom status. The unique username only allows lowercase letters, numbers, underscores and periods, so it cannot use a fancy font. Discord also lowercases and hyphenates text channel names, so styled letters may not survive there. The Discord Font Changer shows a preview and checks username rules.

Which Style to Use Where

  • Bold, Sans Bold and Small Caps are the easiest to read, so they suit names and short bios.
  • Gothic and Script give an elegant, moody look. They work best for short headings, because thin letters get hard to read on small screens.
  • Bubble is playful and works well for casual nicknames.
  • Double-Struck, Monospace and Fullwidth give a clean, tech or retro feel for display names and titles.
  • Bold Italic and Italic suit captions and a clean, professional profile title.
  • Always preview your chosen style in the actual app before posting, since very old devices occasionally show unsupported characters as blank boxes.

Common Problems and Fixes

  • Letters show as boxes. The device does not have a symbol for that character. Try Bold or Sans Bold, or update the app and your phone.
  • The text gets cut off. Styled letters are wider and may count as more than one character. Shorten the text.
  • Copy does not work. Some in-app browsers block the clipboard. Open the page in Chrome or Safari, or press and hold the text to copy it by hand.
  • The username field rejects it. Usernames only accept plain characters. Paste the styled text into the display name or bio instead.
  • People cannot find me in search. Search matches plain letters. Keep your username plain and use the fancy style for the name or bio.
  • My numbers did not change. Some styles have no number symbols, so digits stay plain. Bold, Sans Bold, Double-Struck, Monospace, Bubble and Fullwidth do convert digits.
  • Screen readers read it strangely. They can read these symbols as odd characters or skip them, so keep important information in plain text.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I change the font on Instagram without an app?

Yes. You do not need to install anything. Font Craft runs in your browser: type your text, copy a style and paste it into Instagram.

Why can't I use a fancy font in my Instagram or TikTok username?

Usernames only accept plain characters: letters, numbers, periods and underscores on Instagram, and letters, numbers, underscores and periods on TikTok. Fancy fonts go in the display name or bio instead.

Do fancy fonts work on Android and iPhone?

Modern versions of Android and iOS support most of these symbols, so styled text normally shows correctly. If a style shows boxes, try Bold or Sans Bold.

Which font style is most readable for a bio?

Bold and Sans Bold are the most readable at small sizes. Script and Gothic look striking but can be hard to read on small screens, so use them for short names or headings.
